# Prototype 0.3 — Timing production

Progression update: Timing now requires 500 lifetime XP in addition to its
experiment toggle. See [Prototype05.md](Prototype05.md); the rules below record
the accepted timing-game prototype.

Status: current prototype accepted after the user's initial playtest; further
iteration is deferred. Long-term reward balance and skill improvement remain open.

The user revised 0.3 to test a small skill game inside the economy before building
a separate tactical mode or avatar. Resource A Production now offers Passive
and Timing modes. Existing Standard/Fast Conversion remains available.

## Play

Open `Assets/Scenes/Prototype01.unity` and enter Play Mode. In Production, click
**Mode: Passive** to select Timing. A white marker moves back and forth through
the bar. Click anywhere on the bar when the marker is inside **SUCCESS**, aiming
for the smaller **CRITICAL** section in its center.

- A sweep takes 1.5 seconds in each direction. Success occupies the middle 20%;
  critical occupies the middle 6%, nested inside success.
- One click is accepted per sweep. A miss produces nothing; try again on the next
  sweep. There is no session attempt limit or diminishing reward.
- Success produces two seconds of passive A output for the currently assigned
  Production capacity and efficiency. Critical gives 1.5x that reward (three
  passive seconds). Outside success gives zero. At the initial 3 capacity and
  level 0, success gives 12 A and critical gives 18 A.
- Timing replaces automatic A production. Conversion and Development continue,
  consuming the A generated by hits. Hits do not directly grant Credits or XP.
- Hits/attempts, reward size, readiness and the last hit/miss are shown below the
  bar. Upgrade effects change from A/second to A/hit while Timing is selected.
- Timing runs at 1x. Selecting it changes test speed to 1x and disables 5x/20x
  until Passive is selected again. Pause freezes both economy and marker and
  prevents strikes. Production requires at least one assigned capacity.
- Click **Mode: Timing** to resume Passive. This preserves inventories, upgrades,
  timing position and the current sweep's consumed attempt. Returning to Timing
  cannot reset an attempt. Passive freezes the marker's elapsed challenge time.

The management panel scrolls when the expanded game makes it taller than the
window. Pause and speed controls remain at the bottom.

## Rules and scope

`Economy` owns mode validation, marker position, hit testing, attempt tracking and
reward calculation. Success is inclusive from position 4,000 through 6,000;
critical takes priority from 4,700 through 5,300, also inclusive, on a 0–10,000
scale. The last grade is committed atomically with the reward and attempt;
rejected strikes preserve it. Explicit integer milliseconds drive the timing challenge;
the economy still advances through 0.1-second ticks. Unity schedules those inputs
using frame time and renders the same position used by hit testing.

Invalid, repeated and workerless strikes cannot create A. Checked overflow leaves
reward and attempt state unchanged. UI does not assign resource balances.
This is a local prototype; explicit timing inputs are not trusted server time or
proof of human input. Production rewards will need authoritative validation when
the backend is introduced. No networking, authentication or security was changed.

The **Enable Timing Production** checkbox on `PrototypeController` disables this
experiment when set before Play Mode. It defaults to enabled in presentation;
Core defaults to the disabled experiment unless `timingProductionEnabled: true`
is passed to its constructor. Disable both experiment checkboxes for the 0.1 UI.

Sessions are unsaved. Stop Play Mode before editing scripts, since domain reload
can discard the local simulation. Reward rates, sweep speed and target size are
experimental. Perfect repeated hits yield more A than passive output, but misses,
stockpiles and downstream limits affect the benefit; balance is not established.

## Manual evaluation

Try several forward and reverse sweeps, deliberately hit and miss, and reassign
Production capacity. Confirm clicks match the displayed marker and that feedback
is easy to read. Compare active hits with leaving Passive running, then decide
whether switching feels voluntary and enjoyable. A separate tactical scene remains
a later option; this test does not establish long-term active/idle balance.

October 29 playtest feedback: the user found the current version good enough to
keep and approved committing it. Active timing draws attention away from managing
the idle economy, creating a useful reason to switch between modes. It also makes
upgrade decisions more interesting. This is positive initial feedback, not a
long-term balance or repeated-attempt improvement study.

## Ideas for later
